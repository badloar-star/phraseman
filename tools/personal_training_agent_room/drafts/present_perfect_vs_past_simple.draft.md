# present_perfect_vs_past_simple

Jesse rework pass: Present Perfect vs Past Simple.

Scope:
- One contrast only: result/experience now vs finished past time.
- Present Perfect side: `have lost`, `Have you ever tried`, `have never been`, `has already finished`, `haven't finished yet`.
- Past Simple side: `lost yesterday`, `Did you try yesterday`, `finished last week`, `arrived two days ago`, `went in 2020`.
- Do not expand into full Present Perfect questions/negatives; that belongs to `present_perfect_questions_negatives`.
- Do not expand into `for/since`; that belongs to `present_perfect_for_since`.

Required traps:
- `pp_vs_past_easy_002`: wrong `have lost` must explain `Past Simple`.
- `pp_vs_past_contrast_001`: wrong `Did` must point to `Have you ever tried`.
- `pp_vs_past_contrast_004`: wrong `has finished` must point back to `finished`.
- `pp_vs_past_mixed_006`: wrong `I have never was to London, but I have went to Dublin in 2020.` must point to `went`.

Learner-facing copy rule:
- Russian and Ukrainian copy avoids internal grammar jargon such as `subject`, `object`, `base verb`, `main verb`, `auxiliary`, and `modal`.
