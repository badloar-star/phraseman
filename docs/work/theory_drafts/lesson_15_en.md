**Screen title:** Theory · Plans and arrangements

---

## 01 · What you will learn to say *(open from the start)*

**body**
In this lesson you learn to talk about the future: a plan you have made, something about to happen, a meeting you have fixed. There are two ways to say it, and you already know half of each.

**examples** 🔊
| en | meaning | hi |
|---|---|---|
| I am going to descale the kettle. | my plan, I have decided | going to |
| The sink is going to overflow. | I can see it is about to happen | going to |
| I am meeting the carer tomorrow. | we have fixed a time | am meeting |

---

## 02 · going to — a plan you have decided *(open from the start)*

**body**
When you have already decided to do something, say am / is / are going to + the action as in the dictionary: I am going to repaint the wall. The clip and to are both needed.

**formula**
`who` · `am / is / are going to` · `action as in the dictionary`

**examples** 🔊
| en | meaning | hi |
|---|---|---|
| I am going to descale the kettle. | I have decided to clean it | going to |
| She is going to repaint the wall. | her plan for the wall | going to |
| We are going to alter the dress. | we plan to change its size | going to |
| I am going to hang the bunting. | I plan to put up the flags | going to |

**fix**
- ~~I going to descale the kettle.~~ → I am going to descale the kettle.
- ~~I am going descale the kettle.~~ → I am going to descale the kettle.
- ~~I am going to descaling the kettle.~~ → I am going to descale the kettle.

**drill · choice**
- phrase: `She is going ___ repaint the wall.`
- options: `to` · `—` · `for`
- answer: `to`
- why: After going you always need to.

## 03 · going to — you can see it coming

**body**
Use the same form when the signs are already in front of you: the water is at the edge — The sink is going to overflow; the tower is leaning — The tower is going to topple.

**examples** 🔊
| en | meaning | hi |
|---|---|---|
| The sink is going to overflow. | water is at the edge | going to |
| The tower is going to topple. | it is leaning badly | going to |
| The shelf is going to collapse. | it is bending | going to |
| The jumper is going to shrink. | it is in a hot wash | going to |
| The boards are going to rot. | they are wet all the time | going to |

**fix**
- ~~The tower going to topple.~~ → The tower is going to topple.
- ~~The boards is going to rot.~~ → The boards are going to rot.

**drill · binary**
- question: The water in the sink is right at the edge and the tap is still on. What do you say?
- A: `The sink overflows.`
- B: `The sink is going to overflow.`
- correct: B
- explain: You can see it is about to happen: is going to.

## 04 · An arrangement: I am meeting… tomorrow

**body**
When you have agreed with someone and there is a time — tomorrow at six — use the form from lesson 9: am / is / are + -ing and the time. I am meeting the carer tomorrow. Without the time it sounds like now.

**formula**
`who` · `am / is / are` · `action + ing` · `when`

**examples** 🔊
| en | meaning | hi |
|---|---|---|
| I am meeting the carer tomorrow. | the time is fixed | am meeting |
| We are meeting the babysitter at six tomorrow. | agreed time: six | are meeting |
| We are visiting a car boot sale on Sunday. | it is in our diary | are visiting |
| They are meeting at the book club tonight. | the club meets tonight | are meeting |

**fix**
- ~~I meet the carer tomorrow.~~ *(we have agreed)* → I am meeting the carer tomorrow.
- ~~I am meet the carer tomorrow.~~ → I am meeting the carer tomorrow.

---

## 05 · Plan or arrangement?

**body**
going to — you decided it yourself: I am going to renew my pass. -ing with a time — you have agreed it with someone, there is a date: We are paying for our membership on Friday. Both are about the future; the difference is whether there is an agreement.

**examples** 🔊
| en | meaning | hi |
|---|---|---|
| I am going to renew my pass. | my own decision | going to |
| I am bringing my appointment card tomorrow. | the appointment is booked | am bringing |
| I am going to attend a christening. | my own decision | going to |
| We are attending a hen party on Saturday. | it is booked for Saturday | are attending |

**drill · choice**
- phrase: `We ___ the quince tomorrow.` *(we agreed with the neighbour, he is waiting)*
- options: `are collecting` · `collect` · `are going collect`
- answer: `are collecting`
- why: There is an agreement and a time: am / is / are + -ing.

## 06 · Mistakes almost everyone makes

**fix**
- ~~I going to descale the kettle.~~ → I am going to descale the kettle.
- ~~I am going descale the kettle.~~ → I am going to descale the kettle.
- ~~I am going to descaling the kettle.~~ → I am going to descale the kettle.
- ~~He are going to stumble.~~ → He is going to stumble.
- ~~I meet the carer tomorrow.~~ *(we have agreed)* → I am meeting the carer tomorrow.

**tip**
going to is four parts: who + am / is / are + going + to, then the action as in the dictionary. Do not drop any of them.

## 07 · What to take into practice

**body**
Your own plan — am / is / are going to + action. You can see it coming — going to again. Agreed with someone, with a time — am / is / are + -ing + when.

**examples** 🔊
| en | meaning | hi |
|---|---|---|
| We are going to apply an undercoat. | our plan for the wall | going to |
| The scarf is going to unravel. | the threads are coming loose | going to |
| They are meeting the cleaner tomorrow morning. | the time is fixed | are meeting |
