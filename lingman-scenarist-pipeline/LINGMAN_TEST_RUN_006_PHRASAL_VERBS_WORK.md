## 6. Full Teleprompter Script

### Hook

If your work English sounds like this:

```text
I will respond later.
I will investigate this.
We need to postpone the meeting.
```

It is correct.

But it may sound a little stiff.

A little translated.

Like your sentence came to the meeting wearing a tie it did not choose.

Natural work English often says:

```text
I'll get back to you.
I'll look into this.
Let's put off the meeting.
```

Those are phrasal verbs.

And today we are not going to memorize them like a sad vocabulary list.

We are going to use them as work-message buttons.

### The Work-Message Model

In work messages, phrasal verbs often answer one question:

```text
What action am I trying to do?
```

If you want to reply later:

```text
get back to
```

If you want to investigate:

```text
look into
```

If you want to review:

```text
go over
```

If you want to schedule:

```text
set up
```

If you want to delay:

```text
put off
```

If you want to mention:

```text
bring up
```

If you want to check again after time passes:

```text
follow up
```

These are not random monsters from the grammar basement.

They are work actions.

### Phrase 1 - Get Back To

Use:

```text
get back to
```

when you will reply later.

Message:

```text
I'll get back to you by the end of the day.
```

More polite:

```text
Let me check and get back to you.
```

With timing:

```text
I'll get back to you tomorrow morning.
```

Do not write:

```text
I will answer you later.
```

It is understandable.

But it sounds less natural in many work contexts.

### Phrase 2 - Look Into

Use:

```text
look into
```

when you will investigate or check a problem.

Message:

```text
I'll look into this and let you know.
```

More specific:

```text
I'll look into the issue and get back to you.
```

This phrase is beautiful because it sounds active without sounding dramatic.

You are not launching a formal investigation.

You are checking what is going on.

### Phrase 3 - Go Over

Use:

```text
go over
```

when you review something.

Message:

```text
Let's go over the document tomorrow.
```

Another:

```text
I went over your notes and added a few comments.
```

This is more natural than:

```text
I reviewed your notes.
```

Not always better.

But often warmer and more conversational.

### Phrase 4 - Set Up

Use:

```text
set up
```

when you arrange a meeting, call, account, or process.

Message:

```text
Can we set up a quick call?
```

Another:

```text
I'll set up the meeting for Thursday.
```

Another:

```text
We need to set up access for the new user.
```

This phrase is a workhorse.

Not glamorous.

Very useful.

Like a spreadsheet with social skills.

### Phrase 5 - Put Off

Use:

```text
put off
```

when you delay something.

Message:

```text
Can we put off the meeting until Friday?
```

More formal:

```text
Can we postpone the meeting until Friday?
```

Important register note:

```text
put off
```

is normal and conversational.

```text
postpone
```

is more formal.

Both are useful.

The point is to choose the right temperature for your workplace.

### Phrase 6 - Bring Up

Use:

```text
bring up
```

when you mention a topic.

Message:

```text
I'll bring this up in the next meeting.
```

Another:

```text
Thanks for bringing this up.
```

This is much more natural than:

```text
Thank you for mentioning this topic.
```

That sentence is correct.

It also sounds like it was printed on office furniture.

### Phrase 7 - Follow Up

Use:

```text
follow up
```

when you check again after a previous message, task, or meeting.

Message:

```text
I just wanted to follow up on my last message.
```

Shorter:

```text
Just following up on this.
```

With a task:

```text
I'll follow up with the team and let you know.
```

This phrase is everywhere in work English.

If work messages had background music, "follow up" would be playing quietly all day.

Because if you only memorize:


You need the full chunk:

```text

Can we put off the meeting until Friday?

```

### Register Check

One warning.

Phrasal verbs often sound natural and conversational.

But sometimes you need a more formal version.

Neutral:

```text
I'll look into this.
```

More formal:

```text
I'll investigate this.
```

Neutral:

```text
Can we put off the meeting?
```

More formal:

```text
Can we postpone the meeting?
```

Neutral:

```text
I'll get back to you.
```

More formal:

```text
I'll respond by tomorrow.
```

So do not think:

```text
phrasal verb = always better.
```

Think:

```text
phrasal verb = often more natural in everyday work messages.
```

Professor Lingman verdict:

```text
Natural English is not always less formal. It is better matched to the room.
```
