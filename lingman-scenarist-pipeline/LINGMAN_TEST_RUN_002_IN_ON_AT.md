## 1. Strategic Summary

This episode attacks one high-confusion grammar trap:

```text
in / on / at
```

The angle is not:

```text
memorize three Russian translations.
```

The angle is:

```text
Stop translating prepositions as words. Start seeing them as camera positions.
```

Core mental model:

```text
IN = inside a space or period
ON = on a surface, line, system, or day
AT = exact point, place, address, or moment
```

One strong thing:

```text
In, on, and at are not translations. They are tiny cameras.
```

## 6. Full Teleprompter Script

### Hook

If you learned:

```text
in = в
on = на
at = у
```

I understand why you suffer.

That system looks simple.

It is also a tiny grammar trap wearing a nice shirt.

Because English prepositions are not clean Russian translations.

They are more like tiny cameras.

One camera looks inside a space:

```text
in
```

One camera looks at a surface, a line, a system, or a day:

```text
on
```

One camera points to an exact spot:

```text
at
```

Today I will not give you a giant table that looks like it was designed to end joy.

I will give you a simple model.

Not perfect for every idiom.

But strong enough to fix many mistakes immediately.

### Promise Receipt

Look.

Why do we say:

```text
in a room
on the table
at the door
```

Because:

```text
room = space you are inside
table = surface
door = exact point
```

Now time:

```text
in April
on Monday
at 5 o'clock
```

Because:

```text
April = period
Monday = day on the calendar
5 o'clock = exact point in time
```

That is the receipt.

You clicked for IN / ON / AT.

Here is the model:

```text
IN = container
ON = surface or calendar line
AT = point
```

Do not translate first.

Look at the relationship.

Professor Lingman verdict:

```text
If your brain asks "how do I translate в?", tell it to sit down and observe the geometry.
```

### The Three-Camera Model

Imagine English has three small cameras.

Camera one:

```text
IN
```

This camera zooms out and sees a container.

Not always a literal box.

A space.

A period.

A situation.

```text
in a room
in London
in April
in the morning
in trouble
```

You are inside something.

Camera two:

```text
ON
```

This camera sees contact, surface, line, system, or day.

```text
on the table
on the wall
on the road
on Monday
on the internet
on the phone
```

You are on a surface.

Or on a line.

Or on a system.

Or on a day sitting on the calendar like a little square with responsibilities.

Camera three:

```text
AT
```

This camera points.

It does not care about inside.

It does not care about surface.

It says:

"There. Exact point."

```text
at the door
at the station
at home
at 5 o'clock
at the meeting
at 24 Oxford Street
```

AT is the finger point of English.

Small.

Precise.

Slightly bossy.

### Place Examples

Now we test the model.

```text
I am in the kitchen.
```

Kitchen is a space.

You are inside it.

```text
The keys are on the table.
```

Table is a surface.

The keys are touching it.

```text
I am at the door.
```

Door is treated as a point.

Not a container.

Not a lifestyle.

A point.

Now:

```text
I am in London.
```

London is a large area.

You are inside it.

```text
I am at the airport.
```

Airport can be a large building, yes.

But here we often use it as a point on a map.

Your friend asks:

"Where are you?"

You say:

```text
I'm at the airport.
```

Meaning:

"That is my location."

Not:

"Let me describe the interior architecture of Terminal 2."

### Time Examples

Now time.

```text
in 2026
in May
in the morning
```

These are periods.

You are inside the period.

```text
on Monday
on May 20
on my birthday
```

These are days or dates.

They sit on the calendar.

```text
at 5 o'clock
at noon
at night
```

These are points or fixed time phrases.

Yes, "at night" is annoying.

English looked at night, a whole dark period, and said:

"Point."

We do not fight every phrase in the street.

We mark it as a fixed chunk and practice it.

That is not weakness.

That is strategy.

### Micro-Test

Quick test.

Choose the natural phrase:

```text
1. in Monday / on Monday / at Monday
2. in the table / on the table / at the table
3. in 7 o'clock / on 7 o'clock / at 7 o'clock
4. in the internet / on the internet / at the internet
5. in home / on home / at home
```

Answers:

```text
1. on Monday
2. on the table
3. at 7 o'clock
4. on the internet
5. at home
```

And now you see the danger.

The model helps.

But some phrases are fixed by usage.

So we do two things:

We understand the pattern.

Then we practice the chunks.

This is how adults survive prepositions with dignity.

### Common Traps

Trap one:

```text
in Monday
```

No.

Monday is a day.

```text
on Monday
```

Trap two:

```text
in home
```

No.

Normal phrase:

```text
at home
```

Do not argue with it.

Put it in the fixed chunk box.

Trap three:

```text
in the internet
```

Usually:

```text
on the internet
```

Because English treats the internet like a system or platform.

Trap four:

```text
at the car
```

If you are inside the car:

```text
in the car
```

But public transport is often:

```text
on the bus
on the train
on the plane
```

Why?

Because English is imagining a transport system you can board and move around in.

Is this perfectly logical?

No.

Is it learnable?

Yes.

As chunks.

Professor Lingman verdict:

```text
Prepositions are small words with executive power. Respect them, but do not let them run your life.
```

## 9. Description

IN, ON, and AT are not simple translations of "в", "на", and "у".

If you keep choosing the wrong English preposition, the problem may be that you are translating words instead of seeing the relationship. In this lesson, Professor Lingman explains IN / ON / AT with a simple camera model:

```text
IN = inside a space or time period
ON = surface, system, line, day, or date
AT = exact point, place, address, or moment
```

You will learn why we say:

```text
in the morning
on Monday
at 5 o'clock
at home
on the internet
in the car
on the bus
```

You will also see why some phrases must be practiced as chunks instead of forced into one perfect rule.

After watching, practice common IN / ON / AT phrase chunks in Phraseman so the natural phrase comes out automatically.
