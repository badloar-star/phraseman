**Screen title:** Theory · Questions and «not» about habits

## 01 · What you will learn to say *(open at start)*

**body**
In lesson 6 you said what people do: I work, she cooks. Now you will learn to ask questions — «do you work in a factory?», «where do you park?» —, to say «not» — «I don't gossip» — and to add usually and often.

**examples** 🔊
| en | meaning | hi |
|---|---|---|
| Do you work in a factory? | Is your job in a factory? | Do |
| Does the salon close late? | Is the salon open in the evening? | Does |
| I don't gossip. | I don't talk about people behind their backs. | don't |
| Where do you park? | Where do you leave your car? | Where do |
| I usually recycle. | Most days, I sort my rubbish. | usually |

---

## 02 · Questions start with Do *(open at start)*

**body**
In many languages you ask a question just with your voice. In English, a question about actions starts with a small Do: Do you work in a factory? The action itself doesn't change.

**formula**
`Do` · `you / they / we` · `action` · `?`

**examples** 🔊
| en | meaning | hi |
|---|---|---|
| Do you work in a factory? | Is your job in a factory? | Do |
| Do they send packages? | Is sending packages what they do? | Do |
| Do you have a menu? | Can I see what food you have? | Do |
| Do you work at night? | Is your job at night? | Do |
| Do they check tickets here? | Will someone look at my ticket here? | Do |

**fix**
- ~~You work in a factory?~~ → Do you work in a factory?
- ~~Are you work in a factory?~~ → Do you work in a factory?
- ~~Have you a menu?~~ → Do you have a menu?

**drill · word_bank**
- task: Ask if the person works in a factory.
- answer: `Do` · `you` · `work` · `in` · `a` · `factory`
- slot labels: none (the sentence is long)
- extra tiles: `Are` · `works`

## 03 · Does — for he, she and one person

**body**
When you ask about he, she, it or one person — the barber, the fan — use Does instead of Do. The s from the action (he works) moves to does and disappears from the action: Does he work?

**formula**
`Does` · `he / she / it / one person` · `action without s` · `?`

**examples** 🔊
| en | meaning | hi |
|---|---|---|
| Does the barber work here? | Is this where the barber works? | Does |
| Does the fan work? | Is the fan broken or not? | Does |
| Does he play chess? | Is chess a game he plays? | Does |
| Does she play tennis? | Is tennis a sport she plays? | Does |
| Does the cafe serve sushi? | Can you get sushi in this cafe? | Does |

**fix**
- ~~Does he plays chess?~~ → Does he play chess?
- ~~Do the fan work?~~ → Does the fan work?
- ~~Does she plays tennis?~~ → Does she play tennis?

**drill · choice**
- sentence: `Does the metro ___ late?`
- options: `run` · `runs` · `running`
- answer: `run`
- why: The s already moved to does, so the action has no s.

## 04 · Do or the clip?

**body**
If the question has an action, use Do or Does: Do they fix lamps? If there is no action and you ask who or what someone is, use the clip, like in lesson 2: Are you a plumber? Don't use Do and the clip together.

**examples** 🔊
| en | meaning | hi |
|---|---|---|
| Are you a plumber? | Is fixing pipes your job? | Are |
| Do they fix lamps? | Is fixing lamps what they do? | Do |
| Is the lesson difficult? | Is the lesson hard? | Is |
| Does the vet start early? | Does the animal doctor begin work early? | Does |

**fix**
- ~~Do you a plumber?~~ → Are you a plumber?
- ~~Are they fix lamps?~~ → Do they fix lamps?

**drill · binary**
- question: You want to know if the shop fixes lamps. Which is correct?
- A: `Are they fix lamps?`
- B: `Do they fix lamps?`
- correct: B
- explanation: There is an action (fix), so use Do.

## 05 · «Not»: don't and doesn't

**body**
To say «not» about actions, put don't before the action — with I, you, we, they. With he, she, it and one person, use doesn't, and the action loses its s again: He doesn't have a house.

**formula**
`who` · `don't / doesn't` · `action without s`

**examples** 🔊
| en | meaning | hi |
|---|---|---|
| I don't gossip. | I don't talk about people behind their backs. | don't |
| I don't brag. | I don't talk about how great I am. | don't |
| I don't have money. | I have no money. | don't |
| He doesn't have a house. | He has no house. | doesn't |
| The horn doesn't work. | The horn makes no sound. | doesn't |

**fix**
- ~~I not gossip.~~ → I don't gossip.
- ~~He don't have a house.~~ → He doesn't have a house.
- ~~He doesn't has a house.~~ → He doesn't have a house.
- ~~The horn doesn't works.~~ → The horn doesn't work.

**drill · spot_slip**
- tiles: `The` · `horn` · `doesn't` · `works`
- wrong: `works` (index 3)
- hint: One word has an extra letter. Tap it.
- explanation: After doesn't, the action has no s: The horn doesn't work.

## 06 · There is no

**body**
To say something isn't here, you can use There is no: There is no courier here. For more than one thing, use There are no. After no, don't use a or any.

**examples** 🔊
| en | meaning | hi |
|---|---|---|
| There is no courier here. | The delivery person isn't here. | no |
| There is no tape here. | I can't find any tape. | no |
| There is no bus in the morning. | Buses don't come in the morning. | no |
| There are no pliers here. | I can't find the pliers. | no |
| There is no guard at the gate. | Nobody is watching the gate. | no |

**fix**
- ~~There is not courier here.~~ → There is no courier here.
- ~~Here no courier.~~ → There is no courier here.
- ~~There is no a courier here.~~ → There is no courier here.

**tip**
There isn't a courier here means the same — you know it from lesson 4. There is no sounds a little firmer.

## 07 · Where and when: Where do…? When does…?

**body**
To ask where or when, put Where or When first. Then it's like a normal question: do or does, who, action. Where do you park? When does the rink close?

**formula**
`Where / When` · `do / does` · `who` · `action` · `?`

**examples** 🔊
| en | meaning | hi |
|---|---|---|
| Where do you work? | What place is your job in? | Where do |
| Where do you park? | Where do you leave your car? | Where do |
| When does the rink close? | What time does the ice rink close? | When does |
| When do you have lunch? | What time do you eat lunch? | When do |
| When does she harvest apples? | What time of year does she pick apples? | When does |

**fix**
- ~~Where you work?~~ → Where do you work?
- ~~When the rink closes?~~ → When does the rink close?
- ~~When does she harvests apples?~~ → When does she harvest apples?

**drill · choice**
- sentence: `When ___ the rink close?`
- options: `do` · `does` · `is`
- answer: `does`
- why: The rink is one thing, like he or she, so does.

## 08 · usually and often — before the action

**body**
usually means most of the time. often means many times. They go right before the action: I usually recycle, I often iron shirts. Not after it: I iron often shirts is wrong.

**formula**
`who` · `usually / often` · `action`

**examples** 🔊
| en | meaning | hi |
|---|---|---|
| I usually recycle. | Most days, I sort my rubbish. | usually |
| I often iron shirts. | Many times, I iron shirts. | often |
| She often dusts the shelves. | Many times, she cleans dust off the shelves. | often |
| I usually unplug the kettle. | Most days, I pull the kettle plug out. | usually |

**fix**
- ~~I iron often shirts.~~ → I often iron shirts.
- ~~She often dust the shelves.~~ → She often dusts the shelves.
- ~~I usually recycles.~~ → I usually recycle.

**tip**
usually and often don't change anything else: after she, the action still gets s — she often dusts.

**drill · binary**
- question: You talk about yourself. Which is correct?
- A: `I iron often shirts.`
- B: `I often iron shirts.`
- correct: B
- explanation: often goes right before the action iron.

## 09 · Do you often…?

**body**
In a question, often stays in the same place: before the action, after who. Do you often forget the key? Does your brother often miss the bus? First Do or Does, then who, then often, then the action.

**examples** 🔊
| en | meaning | hi |
|---|---|---|
| Do you often forget the key? | Do you forget the key many times? | often |
| Does your brother often miss the bus? | Is your brother late for the bus many times? | often |

**fix**
- ~~Do often you forget the key?~~ → Do you often forget the key?
- ~~Does your brother often misses the bus?~~ → Does your brother often miss the bus?

## 10 · Mistakes almost everyone makes

**fix**
- ~~You work in a factory?~~ → Do you work in a factory?
- ~~Are you work here?~~ → Do you work here?
- ~~Does he plays chess?~~ → Does he play chess?
- ~~I not gossip.~~ → I don't gossip.
- ~~He don't have a house.~~ → He doesn't have a house.
- ~~There is not courier here.~~ → There is no courier here.
- ~~Where you work?~~ → Where do you work?
- ~~I iron often shirts.~~ → I often iron shirts.

**tip**
The main rule: the s goes in one place only. If there is does or doesn't, the action has no s. If there is no does, the action gets s after he and she.

## 11 · What to take into practice

**body**
Questions: Do or Does first. «Not»: don't or doesn't before the action. After does and doesn't, no s. «Not here»: There is no. Where and when: Where, When + do / does. usually and often: right before the action.

**examples** 🔊
| en | meaning | hi |
|---|---|---|
| Do they work on weekends? | Are they at work on Saturday and Sunday? | Do |
| Does the coach leave early? | Does the bus go early? | Does |
| He doesn't have a house. | He has no house. | doesn't |
| When does the rink close? | What time does the ice rink close? | When does |
| She often dusts the shelves. | Many times, she cleans dust off the shelves. | often |
