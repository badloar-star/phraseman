# Curriculum Roadmap 33-60

This roadmap keeps Lesson 33 connected to a minimum 60-lesson course expansion.

| Lesson | CEFR | Topic | Role | Prerequisites | Recycles | New skill | Risk | Why here |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 33 | B2 | have/get something done | Focused continuation after Lesson 32 object-result patterns. | Lesson 23 passive voice; Lesson 31 complex object; Lesson 32 mixed review | need/want + object + V3; passive result language; service-result phrases | Arrange an action/result without saying the learner did it personally. | Learners may confuse I repaired it with I had it repaired. | Lesson 32 already introduces need/want + object + V3, so Lesson 33 safely narrows that bridge into one B2 pattern. |
| 34 | B2 | wish / if only | Unreal present and regret without broad C1 overload. | past simple for unreal meaning; modal would; Lesson 32 mixed review | past forms; advice and regret phrases; personal-state sentences | Express unreal present wishes and controlled regrets. | Learners may overuse would after wish with the same subject. | After Lesson 33 result control, the course shifts into unreal meaning before full conditionals. |
| 35 | B2 | third conditional basics | Past unreal cause and result. | wish / if only; past perfect; would have + V3 | regret phrases; past result logic; V3 recognition | Build if + past perfect, would have + V3 sentences. | Learners may mix second and third conditional forms too early. | Wish/regret gives the meaning first; third conditional then adds the full cause-result frame. |
| 36 | B2 | mixed conditionals basics | Controlled present result from past condition. | third conditional basics; second conditional meaning; would + V | past unreal condition; present result phrases; regret logic | Connect a past unreal cause to a present result. | Learners may treat every mixed conditional as free-form tense mixing. | It comes only after third conditional basics, so the timeline shift is explicit and not random. |
| 37 | B2 | present perfect continuous | Duration and visible result. | present perfect; for/since; state vs action verbs | visible result language; duration phrases; current-state descriptions | Explain ongoing activity leading to a present result. | Learners may use it with stative verbs or confuse it with simple present perfect. | After unreal-condition work, the course returns to high-frequency tense control before adding past/future complexity. |
| 38 | B2 | past perfect continuous | Background duration before a past event. | present perfect continuous; past perfect; past time clauses | duration phrases; background context; before/when clauses | Show an activity continuing before another past point. | Learners may overuse it where past simple is enough. | It naturally extends Lesson 37 duration logic into past narrative sequencing. |
| 39 | B2 | future continuous and future perfect | Future process and completion. | future forms; continuous aspect; perfect aspect | time markers; completion logic; process vs result contrast | Separate future activity in progress from future completion by a deadline. | Learners may choose forms by translation instead of time perspective. | After present/past perfect continuous, future aspect completes the B2 tense-aspect set. |
| 40 | B2 | advanced modal deduction | must have, might have, cannot have. | modals; perfect infinitive; V3 forms | past result evidence; certainty phrases; would have + V3 recognition | Make past deductions with modal + have + V3. | Learners may confuse deduction with obligation or advice. | It uses the V3 confidence built in Lessons 33, 35, and 39 before the B2+ nuance block. |
| 41 | B2+ | permission, obligation, and necessity nuance | have to, need to, be supposed to, be allowed to. | basic modals; advanced modal deduction; need/want forms | need to; have to; allowed to; supposed to | Choose obligation, expectation, necessity, and permission with nuance. | Learners may flatten all forms into must/can. | It starts B2+ with meaning nuance, using modal control from Lesson 40. |
| 42 | B2+ | concession clauses | although, even though, despite, in spite of. | compound sentences; contrast language; gerunds/noun phrases | however contrast; although clauses; despite + noun/V-ing | Express contrast inside one sentence with clause vs phrase patterns. | Learners may write despite that or although of. | After modal nuance, the course expands sentence architecture without jumping to C1. |
| 43 | B2+ | purpose and result clauses | so that, in order to, such that, so/such. | concession clauses; infinitives; result language | so; because; to + verb; result phrases | Distinguish purpose from result in longer sentences. | Learners may confuse so that purpose with so result. | It pairs with Lesson 42: contrast first, purpose/result next. |
| 44 | B2+ | reason and contrast connectors | therefore, however, nevertheless, whereas. | concession clauses; purpose and result clauses; paragraph connectors | because/so; although/however; cause-result sequencing | Link ideas across clauses and sentences with formal connectors. | Learners may use connectors mechanically without matching logic. | It consolidates Lessons 42-43 into discourse-level control. |
| 45 | B2+ | noun clauses | what I need, where we met, whether it works. | question word order; embedded clauses; object complements | what/where/whether; reported speech word order; object slots | Use whole clauses as subjects, objects, and complements. | Learners may keep question word order inside noun clauses. | After connector work, learners are ready for clause-as-unit grammar. |
| 46 | B2+ | reported questions advanced | Embedded questions and reporting verbs. | noun clauses; reported speech; question word order | where we met; whether it works; reported speech anchors | Report questions with embedded word order and tense control. | Learners may write asked where did he go. | Noun clauses make embedded question structure teachable instead of memorized. |
| 47 | B2+ | reporting verbs with patterns | admit, deny, suggest, warn, promise. | reported questions advanced; gerunds; infinitives | reported speech; that clauses; to + verb and V-ing patterns | Select the correct grammar pattern after common reporting verbs. | Learners may attach every reporting verb to a that clause. | It broadens reporting after Lesson 46 while keeping pattern choice explicit. |
| 48 | B2+ | passive reporting structures | It is said that, he is believed to. | passive voice; reporting verbs with patterns; to-infinitive clauses | passive forms; reported speech; formal claim language | Build formal impersonal reporting structures. | Learners may mix it is said to he or he is said that. | It is the formal passive extension of Lessons 46-47 before participle compression. |
| 49 | B2+ | participle clauses basics | Walking home, having finished, seen from here. | passive reporting structures; V-ing clauses; V3 passive meaning | V-ing modifiers; V3 result language; time/reason clauses | Compress time, reason, and passive meaning into participle clauses. | Learners may create dangling participles. | It uses strong V3/passive control from Lessons 33 and 48. |
| 50 | B2+ | reduced relative clauses | people living here, documents checked yesterday. | relative clauses; participle clauses basics; active/passive participles | relative clauses; V-ing active modifiers; V3 passive modifiers | Reduce relative clauses into active and passive participle modifiers. | Learners may confuse reduced relatives with full participle clauses. | It completes the B2+ clause-compression block before C1 emphasis and inversion. |
| 51 | C1 | inversion basics | Never have I, only then did I. | conditionals; modal auxiliaries; reported clauses; negative adverbials | auxiliary questions; negative emphasis; only/never structures | Use subject-auxiliary inversion after fronted negative or limiting adverbials. | Learners may invert without an inversion trigger. | It opens C1 only after conditionals, modal control, and clause work are stable. |
| 52 | C1 | cleft sentences | What I need is, It was John who. | noun clauses; relative clauses; sentence focus | what clauses; it was...who; emphasis patterns | Move important information into focused cleft structures. | Learners may overbuild heavy sentences with unclear focus. | After inversion, the course teaches a second C1 emphasis tool with less word-order risk. |
| 53 | C1 | emphasis with do and auxiliaries | I do understand, he did call. | auxiliary verbs; cleft sentences; contrastive stress | do-support; modal/auxiliary choice; correction phrases | Add spoken/written emphasis with do and auxiliary stress. | Learners may use emphatic do where tense or register makes it awkward. | It follows heavier C1 emphasis structures with a compact high-frequency emphasis tool. |
| 54 | C1 | hedging and cautious claims | seems to, appears to, is likely to. | modal deduction; passive reporting structures; formal claim language | seems; likely; reported claims; certainty scale | Make cautious claims instead of overstrong statements. | Learners may hedge every sentence or mix certainty levels. | It builds directly from deduction and formal reporting into C1 academic/professional stance. |
| 55 | C1 | advanced adjective and noun complements | aware of, responsible for, the fact that. | noun clauses; preposition patterns; hedging and cautious claims | that clauses; preposition complements; formal noun phrases | Attach clauses and prepositional complements to adjectives and nouns accurately. | Learners may translate complements word-for-word from L1. | Hedging needs precise complement patterns, so this stabilizes the grammar behind C1 claims. |
| 56 | C1 | advanced preposition patterns | dependent on, associated with, involved in. | advanced adjective and noun complements; passive forms; formal vocabulary | adjective + preposition; noun + preposition; passive adjective patterns | Control fixed preposition patterns in formal and professional phrases. | Learners may memorize lists without phrase-level production. | It follows complement work so prepositions are learned as grammar slots, not isolated words. |
| 57 | C1 | formal cause and consequence | thereby, hence, consequently, due to. | reason and contrast connectors; purpose/result clauses; advanced preposition patterns | therefore/however; due to; result clauses; formal connectors | State formal cause and consequence across clauses and sentences. | Learners may overuse formal connectors in casual phrases. | It upgrades the B2+ connector block with C1 register and precision. |
| 58 | C1 | stance and evaluation | arguably, admittedly, presumably, supposedly. | hedging and cautious claims; formal cause and consequence; discourse connectors | cautious claims; contrast markers; certainty adverbs | Signal stance, evaluation, and source attitude in compact adverbials. | Learners may choose stance adverbs by translation, not speaker attitude. | It completes the C1 claim-making sequence before integrated review. |
| 59 | C1 | advanced mixed review 33-58 | Integrated production with controlled complexity. | lessons 33-58; B2+ clause control; C1 emphasis and stance | 33-58 target patterns; mixed production; error repair | Combine causative, clauses, reporting, inversion, emphasis, and stance in one controlled set. | Learners may pass recognition but fail mixed production. | It is the stress test before the final bridge review, not a new grammar overload. |
| 60 | C1 | final C1 bridge review | Course capstone and readiness for future C1 modules. | advanced mixed review 33-58; all Lesson 33-59 production gates | 33-59 course spine; high-risk patterns; personal training weak spots | Demonstrate readiness for later C1 modules through integrated review and self-correction. | Learners may need more recycling before moving into denser C1 material. | It caps the 33-60 expansion and decides what future C1 modules should recycle first. |
