<a id="be-present-i-am"></a>
## Present `be` with `I`

- Cambridge Grammar, [Verb: be](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/grammar/british-grammar/verb-be), gives `(I) am` as the present form and describes uses of `be` for qualities, states, feelings, situations, and place. The [British Council reference](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/english-grammar-reference/verb-be) independently gives the same present-tense form.
- Supported claim: in the complete lesson phrases `I am tired/busy/late/sorry`, `am` links subject `I` to the following description.
- Boundary: this does not claim that every occurrence of `I` in English must be followed by `am`.

<a id="pronoun-i"></a>

<a id="tire-noun-verb"></a>
## `tire` is not only a noun

- Oxford Learner's Dictionaries, [tire](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/tire_1), records the verb sense “to become tired” or “to make somebody tired”.
- Cambridge Dictionary, [tire](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/tire), records both the verb and the US noun meaning a rubber wheel covering.
- Supported correction: `tire` does not name the adjective state required by the prompt, but it must not be taught as meaning only `шина`.
