# L01 S01 source evidence — `I am` + place/state

Status: retained source evidence for the bounded S01 candidate. This file records what each source supports; it is not a reviewer verdict and does not authorize release.

<a id="be-present-i-am"></a>
## Present `be` with `I`

- Cambridge Grammar, [Be](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/be-expressions-with-be), lists the present forms as `(I) am`, `(he, she, it) is`, and `(you, we, they) are. It also states that `be` describes temporary or permanent qualities and states, feelings, situations, and place.
- Supported claims in S01: `am` is the present form used with `I`; `be` can link `I` to a place or state description.
- Not supported by this source: any claim that `I am` must always be followed by another word in every context.

<a id="short-answer-i-am"></a>
## `I am` as a short answer

- British Council LearnEnglish, [Present simple: ‘to be’](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/a1-a2/present-simple-be), explicitly gives `Yes, I am` as an affirmative short answer.
- Supported claim in S01: `I am` is grammatical as part of a short answer when the missing information is supplied by the question.
- Context boundary in S01 intro 3: bare `I am` does not provide the requested information “I am happy” in the displayed situation; this is insufficiency for that prompt, not universal ungrammaticality.

<a id="here-adverb"></a>
## `here`

- Cambridge Dictionary, [here](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/here), labels `here` an adverb and defines its A1 place sense as “in, at, or to this place.”
- Supported S01 sense: `here` identifies the speaker’s current place in `I am here`.

<a id="ready-adjective"></a>
## `ready`

- Cambridge Dictionary, [ready](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/ready), labels the “prepared” sense an adjective and includes `ready when you are`, glossed as “I am ready to do what we have arranged.”
- Supported S01 sense: `ready` describes being prepared to start an arranged action.

<a id="fine-adjective"></a>
## `fine`

- Cambridge Dictionary, [fine](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/fine), labels the relevant sense an adjective used after a verb and defines it as satisfactory, healthy, or well.
- Supported S01 sense: `I am fine` can answer a question about how the speaker is doing.

<a id="happy-adjective"></a>
## `happy`

- Cambridge Dictionary, [happy](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/happy), labels `happy` an adjective and defines its A1 sense as feeling or showing pleasure or satisfaction.
- Supported S01 sense: `I am happy` says that the speaker feels pleased or glad.

<a id="pronoun-i"></a>
## Subject pronoun `I`

- Cambridge Grammar, [Personal pronouns](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/pronouns-personal-i-me-you-him-her-etc), identifies `I` as the subject form referring to the speaker or writer and states that subject pronouns normally appear before a verb.
- Supported S01 claim: `I` names the speaker and is the subject in the target statements.

<a id="localized-fine-preposition-u-ru-uk"></a>
## Preposition `у` in the localized `fine` paraphrase

- Russian: Gramota's dictionary entry for [`у`](https://gramota.ru/poisk?dicts%5B0%5D=26&mode=slovari&query=%D1%83&simple=0) labels it a preposition governing the genitive (`у кого-чего`).
- Ukrainian: the official [Ukrainian Orthography](https://mon.gov.ua/storage/app/media/zagalna%20serednya/%202019.pdf) treats prepositions as separate words before pronouns and records the corresponding pronoun forms; the authored phrase is `у мене`.
- Supported bounded claim: the initial RU/UK `у` is a preposition in `у меня / у мене`. Together with the contextual bilingual `fine` rendering above, it introduces the person whose state is described. This anchor does not claim a word-for-word identity with English `fine`.

<a id="localized-fine-pronoun-menya-mene"></a>
## Personal-pronoun forms `меня / мене`

- Russian: Gramota's [personal-pronoun paradigm](https://gramota.ru/biblioteka/spravochniki/russkij-yazyk-kratkij-teoreticheskij-kurs-dlya-shkolnikov/razryady-mestoimeniy-po-grammaticheskim-priznakam) gives `меня` as the genitive/accusative form of personal pronoun `я`.
- Ukrainian: the official [Ukrainian Orthography](https://mon.gov.ua/storage/app/media/zagalna%20serednya/%202019.pdf) gives `мене` as an oblique form of personal pronoun `я`; [Словник.ua: `я`](https://slovnyk.ua/index.php?swrd=%D1%8F) records the same paradigm.
- Supported bounded claim: RU `меня` and UK `мене` are personal-pronoun forms referring to the speaker inside `у меня / у мене`. The separate `I` anchor licenses only the subject form `я` and is not used for these units.

<a id="localized-fine-pronoun-vsyo-vse"></a>
## Generalizing pronouns `всё / все`

- Russian: Gramota's [pronoun rule](https://gramota.ru/uchebnik/pravila/slitnoe-razdelnoe-i-defisnoe-napisanie-mestoimeniy-i-mestoimennykh-narechiy) includes `всё` among pronoun forms.
- Ukrainian: [Словник.ua: `все`](https://slovnyk.ua/index.php?swrd=%D0%B2%D1%81%D0%B5) labels `все / усе` a pronoun and records its substantivized totality/generalizing use.
- Supported bounded claim: RU `всё` and UK `все` are pronouns that generalize the state of affairs in the authored paraphrase. They are not individually translations of English `fine`.

<a id="localized-fine-adverb-khorosho-dobre"></a>
## Evaluative adverbs `хорошо / добре`

- Russian: [Gramota: `хорошо`](https://gramota.ru/meta/khorosho) labels the relevant word an adverb and records positive/well-state senses.
- Ukrainian: [Словник.ua: `добре`](https://slovnyk.ua/index.php?swrd=%D0%B4%D0%BE%D0%B1%D1%80%D0%B5) labels `добре` an adverb and records positive/well-state senses.
- Supported bounded claim: RU `хорошо` and UK `добре` are adverbs giving the positive evaluation in the authored paraphrase. The whole-phrase equivalence to `I am fine` remains supported by the bilingual `fine` anchor, not by a word-for-word equation.
