## 2. Evidence boundary

The CEFR is action-oriented and aligns curriculum, teaching and assessment with
real-world communicative outcomes. Its B1 descriptors require sustained,
straightforward descriptions and narratives across familiar subjects, including
experiences, plans, feelings and reasons. It does not prescribe one universal
English grammar syllabus.

Cambridge B1 Preliminary language specifications and assessment guidance require
control of simple grammatical forms plus attempts at complex forms. The relevant
families include tense contrasts, modal forms, passive forms, infinitives and
verb patterns, and noun, relative and adverb clauses.

Therefore the exact 32-lesson order in this document is a Phraseman
`SYNTHESIS`, not an official CEFR grammar list. It is designed to cover the
high-frequency grammar needed for functional B1 communication while preserving
Phraseman's speaking-first product constraints.

## 3. Non-negotiable curriculum principles

1. Every one of the 32 lessons introduces and develops a new major grammar
   system. There are no checkpoint-only lessons.
2. Checkpoints exist only as sessions inside a lesson.
3. Every new microgrammar operation receives a full session before it can be
   used as assumed knowledge.
4. No activity may use a grammar operation whose prerequisites were not taught
   in an earlier exact session.
5. Every intro teaches the exact grammar later practised in that session.
6. Every practice interaction traces to the session grammar operation, review
   set, phrase family and evidence purpose.
7. New vocabulary exists to create useful, natural examples of the current
   grammar. A word is never introduced merely to satisfy a quota.
8. Important prior grammar and vocabulary return through planned spaced
   retrieval and changed contexts.
9. Recognition is scaffolding, not the endpoint. Each grammar family progresses
   toward lower-support comprehension and spoken production.
10. Structural completeness is not pedagogical approval. A populated table,
    green schema gate or stable hash cannot replace semantic conformance review.

## 4. Exact 32-lesson grammar spine

### Stage A — A1 foundation

| Lesson | Major grammar system | Required scope |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Present `be` affirmative | all subject pronouns; `am/is/are`; full and contracted affirmative forms |
| 2 | Present `be` questions and negatives | negation; yes/no questions; short answers; `who/what/where` with `be` |
| 3 | Nouns and core determiners | `a/an/the`, bounded zero article, singular/plural, `this/that/these/those` |
| 4 | Existence and place | `there is/are`, `some/any`, basic place prepositions |
| 5 | Possession | `have/has`, possessive determiners and pronouns, possessive `'s`, `whose` |
| 6 | Present Simple affirmative | lexical-verb frame, person/number agreement, third-person `-s` |
| 7 | Present Simple questions and negatives | `do/does`, `don't/doesn't`, wh-questions, frequency position |
| 8 | Ability, requests and instructions | `can/can't`, request frames, positive/negative imperative, polite placement |

### Stage B — complete A2 systems

| Lesson | Major grammar system | Required scope |
|---:|---|---|
| 9 | Present Continuous | affirmative, negative and question forms; action happening now |
| 10 | Present Simple vs Continuous | habitual/current/temporary meanings; bounded stative-verb contrast |
| 11 | Past `be` and existence | `was/were`, questions/negatives, `there was/were`, past state/location |
| 12 | Past Simple affirmative | regular and high-frequency irregular whole-word forms |
| 13 | Past Simple questions and negatives | `did/didn't`, base-form restoration, finished-time anchors and sequence |
| 14 | Past Continuous | affirmative/negative/questions; background actions; `when/while` |
| 15 | Planned future | `be going to` intentions and Present Continuous arrangements |
| 16 | `will` future | predictions, spontaneous decisions, promises, offers; future-form contrast |

### Stage C — A2+ to B1 bridge

| Lesson | Major grammar system | Required scope |
|---:|---|---|
| 17 | Countability and quantification | count/non-count, `much/many`, `few/little`, `some/any`, quantity units |
| 18 | Comparison and degree | comparatives, superlatives, `as…as`, `too/enough`, bounded `so/such` |
| 19 | Ability and permission system | `can/could/may/be able to`, present/past ability and polite permission |
| 20 | Obligation, prohibition and advice | `must/have to/should/ought to/needn't/don't have to/mustn't` contrasts |
| 21 | Verb-complement patterns | gerund/infinitive, `want/need/like/prefer`, object + infinitive, infinitive of purpose |
| 22 | Pronoun and reference system | object, possessive and reflexive pronouns; `one/ones`; `another/other` |
| 23 | Present Perfect: experience/result | form, participles, `ever/never/just/already/yet`, short answers |
| 24 | Present Perfect: duration and contrast | `for/since/how long`, bounded Present Perfect Continuous, contrast with Past Simple |

### Stage D — complete functional B1

| Lesson | Major grammar system | Required scope |
|---:|---|---|
| 25 | Past habits and narrative ordering | `used to`, bounded habitual `would`, Past Perfect, `before/after/by the time` |
| 26 | Zero and First Conditional | stable conditions, real future conditions, future time clauses, clause order |
| 27 | Second Conditional and wishes | unreal present/future, `if I were`, bounded `wish/if only` |
| 28 | Passive Voice | present/past/future passive, passive with modal verbs, optional `by` agent |
| 29 | Defining relative clauses | `who/which/that/whose/where`, subject/object roles, bounded object omission |
| 30 | Reported Speech | statements, questions, commands/requests, `say/tell/ask`, bounded backshift |
| 31 | Complex questions and clause linking | indirect questions, question tags, bounded noun clauses, cause/contrast/purpose/result connectors |
| 32 | Probability and deduction | `must/might/may/could/can't`, evidence-based inference, integrated B1 stance and discourse |

`hello`, `name` and other isolated lexical items are not lesson-level grammar
targets. They may appear only when a natural example needs them and all lexical
grounding rules are satisfied.

## 5. Seven chapters per lesson

Each lesson contains seven chapters. The chapter sequence is a dependency-safe
spiral within the lesson:

1. base form and core meaning;
2. person, number, agreement or another structural dimension;
3. negative form or the next required subsystem;
4. question form, alternation or another required subsystem;
5. contrast with a previously learned construction;
6. conversational application across familiar situations;
7. transfer into a changed situation and integration of the lesson system.

The chapter structure is not a fixed semantic template. If a grammar family has
no meaningful negative or question subsystem, those slots receive other
necessary operations from the same family. A chapter may never receive an
unrelated topic just to fill the topology.

## 6. Eight sessions per chapter

Each chapter contains eight full sessions:

1. introduce and explain the first exact microgrammar operation;
2. introduce the next prerequisite-safe operation;
3. introduce a third operation or a necessary diagnostic contrast;
4. guided application with strong-to-medium support fading;
5. diagnostic contextual choice and targeted error repair;
6. listening comprehension and auditory retrieval;
7. spoken production and response;
8. independent chapter checkpoint with no new grammar.

Checkpoint ordinals inside every lesson are `8 / 16 / 24 / 32 / 40 / 48 / 56`.
Session 56 is the final integrated lesson probe. It does not introduce new
grammar. It uses changed contexts and does not copy training prompts as mastery
evidence.

When a chapter contains fewer than three genuine new operations, Sessions 2 or
3 become explicit guided extension/retrieval sessions. They must not invent a
new label for an already identical task.

## 7. Exact session content contract

Every session contains exactly 20 learner interactions:

- slots 1–3: three consecutive intro pages, each with its own embedded check;
- slots 4–20: seventeen practice interactions.

### Intro pages

1. Meaning and communicative purpose.
2. Form and a natural example.
3. The main diagnostic trap and the exact decision rule.

Each page teaches or recentres only the session operation. Questions test the
explained target-language grammar. Tests of Russian/interface-language wording,
random symbols, untranslated metalanguage and future grammar are forbidden.

Retrieval and checkpoint sessions use short recentering pages rather than
pretending to introduce a new rule. They add no unsupported grammar.

### Practice progression

Practice moves from rich support to sparse support:

```text
comprehend meaning
→ recognize form
→ guided choice
→ cued retrieval
→ whole-word construction
→ listening retrieval
→ spoken production
→ changed-context transfer
→ independent evidence
```

Adjacent interactions cannot use the same mode family. The same exact target +
family pair cannot repeat inside one session merely to inflate contact counts.

## 8. Active mode families

Only these six mode-native families may be assigned to new course content:

| Family | Required learning action |
|---|---|
| `phrase_builder` | assemble a phrase from authored whole-word/chunk tiles |
| `listen_choose` | hear real audio and choose the exact locale-native meaning without target-text leakage |
| `listen_build_dictation` | reconstruct a hidden heard phrase from whole words/chunks |
| `context_gap_grammar` | choose one exact form in one meaningful diagnostic gap |
| `speed_match` | match exactly four familiar words to four atomic meanings |
| `scripted_repeat_compare` | hear a model, hold to speak, release to finish and compare honestly |

All six active families appear at least once in session practice, but each
contact must use the family's real native operation. Generic choice, token or
speech widgets relabelled with a family name do not count.

`sound_contrast` is legacy read-only and forbidden for new authoring. Letter
assembly is forbidden in every family. Builders use whole words and meaningful
chunks only.

## 9. Distractors and feedback

Choice-based modes obey the following contract:

1. exactly one contextually and grammatically correct answer;
2. normally three distinct atomic distractors;
3. no slash-separated answer bundles or multiple translations in one option;
4. each distractor represents one plausible, diagnosable learner error;
5. every incorrect option has option-specific feedback explaining why it fails
   in this exact context;
6. no duplicate distractors and no hidden second correct answer;
7. correct-answer position is shuffled deterministically and is not always
   first.

`phrase_builder` and `listen_build_dictation` use retry/verdict and internal
tile diagnostics, not learner-facing choice-feedback copied from multiple
choice.

Speed Match contains exactly four distinct familiar target words and four
atomic meanings. A phrase, an unfamiliar target or more than `4 + 4` visible
buttons produces `HOLD`.

## 10. Lexical policy

Lexicon is subordinate to grammar but remains a planned learning layer.

- An ordinary teaching session introduces zero to two useful new lexical
  senses.
- Zero new senses is valid for deliberate retrieval, contrast, production and
  checkpoint sessions.
- A new sense must be necessary for natural practice of the session grammar.
- A new sense receives meaning, audio and a blocking first-encounter card before
  its first learner interaction.
- The card definition is concise, manually authored in all eight locales and
  cannot depend on unknown target-language words.
- Before scored use, the new sense appears in at least three meaningful
  contacts. This numeric threshold is an owner-approved `PRODUCT_HYPOTHESIS`
  until calibrated from learner data.
- Every introduced sense has explicit future retrieval edges across changed
  contexts. High-value senses recur across later sessions and later lessons.
- Repetition is deliberate; not every session must introduce a word.
- A lexical quota can never justify an unnatural phrase, hidden grammar or
  random item such as the previous `hello/name` packet mismatch.

Already unlocked cards are not shown as blocking overlays on replay. Unlock is
durable from first display, even if the session is not completed.

## 11. Audio, speech and accessibility

- Required audio is prepared before session start.
- Every replay action can start the audio again after completion or interruption.
- Reference audio automatically starts only where the mode contract requires it
  and platform autoplay policy permits it.
- The spoken mode uses the approved hold-press/release lifecycle and does not
  end immediately on press.
- Owner preview provides a skip path for spoken activities so the complete
  session can be reviewed without recording.
- Learner accessibility provides an equivalent assistive action; one noisy
  speech measurement cannot be the sole access gate.
- Motion never blocks immediate interaction. Reduced-motion behavior preserves
  state and meaning.

## 13. Mandatory gates

The rebuild must add deterministic gates for all of the following:

### Scope and coverage

- exactly 32 lessons, 224 chapters and 1,792 packets;
- every lesson owns a new major grammar system;
- the full approved B1 inventory has introduce, practice, production and
  independent-evidence coverage;
- no duplicate lesson system without a declared new learning delta;
- no checkpoint-only lesson.

### Prerequisites and semantic alignment

- every grammar use is reachable through the prerequisite DAG;
- each intro explains the exact session operation;
- every practice interaction traces to the intro operation or explicit review
  set;
- every canonical example contains the grammar it claims to demonstrate;
- examples contain no unexplained future grammar;
- new lexical senses occur naturally in their canonical examples;
- exact packet, source and owner-map bindings share the same fingerprint.

### Lexicon

- new senses have meaning/audio/card/definition data in all eight locales;
- each new sense has grounded contacts before scored use;
- retrieval edges exist and point forward;
- checkpoint sessions introduce no grammar and no scored new lexicon;
- no word exists only to satisfy a count.

### Activities, distractors and feedback

- exactly three intros and seventeen practice interactions per session;
- all six active families are present through real mode-native payloads;
- no adjacent identical family and no repeated exact target + family;
- no letter assembly or active `sound_contrast`;
- Speed Match is exactly four familiar words plus four atomic meanings;
- one correct answer, distinct atomic distractors and option-specific feedback;
- correct-answer positions are not systematically first;
- independent probes use changed prompts and lower support.

### Artifact integrity

- code, documentation, matrices, owner map and package manifest share one exact
  fingerprint;
- any canonical-body change returns approval to `PENDING`;
- the owner map is rebuilt after every status or fingerprint change;
- old superseded approval cannot authorize learner-facing authoring.

No gate may be weakened to preserve an old green result.

## 15. Error handling and stop conditions

The rebuild stops and records `HOLD` when any of these occurs:

- missing or conflicting B1 scope evidence;
- a prerequisite cycle or unreachable operation;
- a lesson without genuinely new grammar;
- a chapter filled with an unrelated topic;
- a packet whose examples do not instantiate its grammar or lexicon;
- an unexplained construction inside intro, task, distractor or feedback;
- a locale gap;
- a repeated/correct distractor;
- old/new fingerprint mismatch;
- learner-source changes before blueprint approval;
- shared-worktree drift in protected future sessions.

The correction is made in the highest incorrect layer and then propagated
downward. Downstream data is never patched to conceal an upstream design error.
