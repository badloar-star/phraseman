**Screen title:** Theory · Things: a, an, the, this, these

---

## 01 · What you will learn to say *(open at start)*

**body**
In this lesson you learn to name the things around you: «it's a chair», «they're tickets», «this charger», «those flowers are fresh», «the mistake is here», «it's a red cup». English uses small words in front of things for this: a, an, the, this, that.

**examples** 🔊
| en | meaning | hi |
|---|---|---|
| It is a chair. | One chair. | a |
| They are tickets. | More than one ticket. | tickets |
| This is a charger. | The charger in my hand. | This |
| Those flowers are fresh. | The flowers over there are fresh. | Those |
| It is a red cup. | One cup, and it is red. | red |

---

## 02 · One thing: put a in front *(open at start)*

**body**
Before one thing, English almost always puts a small a: It is a chair. a means «one», but it's very short and quiet. Without it the sentence sounds broken.

**formula**
`It is` · `a` · `thing`

**examples** 🔊
| en | meaning | hi |
|---|---|---|
| It is a chair. | One chair. | a |
| It's a lamp. | One lamp. | a |
| Is it a table? | You're not sure it's a table. | a |
| It is a key. | One key. | a |
| It is not a window. | It's something else. | a |

**fix**
- ~~It is chair.~~ → It is a chair.
- ~~Is it table?~~ → Is it a table?

**tip**
In questions and with not, a stays in its place: Is it a table? It is not a window.

## 03 · a or an: the first sound decides

**body**
If the word starts with a vowel sound, say an instead of a: an armchair, an oven, an umbrella. It's easier to say: you don't get stuck between two vowel sounds.

**examples** 🔊
| en | meaning | hi |
|---|---|---|
| It is an armchair. | A soft chair with arms. | an |
| Is it an oven? | You cook food in it. | an |
| It's an umbrella. | It keeps rain off you. | an |
| It is an earring. | You wear it on your ear. | an |
| It's an egg. | One egg. | an |
| It is an invoice. | A paper that says what to pay. | an |

**fix**
- ~~It is a oven.~~ → It is an oven.
- ~~It's a egg.~~ → It's an egg.

**drill · choice**
- sentence: `It's ___ umbrella.`
- options: `a` · `an` · `the`
- answer: `an`
- why: umbrella starts with a vowel sound, so an.

## 04 · The sound, not the letter

**body**
Listen to the sound, not the letter. In hour the h is silent, so it starts with a vowel sound: an hour. But uniform and euro start with a «y» sound («you-niform», «you-ro»). That's not a vowel sound, so a.

**examples** 🔊
| en | meaning | hi |
|---|---|---|
| It is an hour. | Sixty minutes. | an |
| Is it a uniform? | Special clothes for work. | a |
| It's a euro. | One euro coin. | a |

**fix**
- ~~It is a hour.~~ → It is an hour.
- ~~It's an euro.~~ → It's a euro.

**tip**
Easy check: say the word out loud. Does it start with an «a, e, i, o, u» sound? Then an. A «y» sound or any other sound? Then a.

## 05 · More than one: add s, drop a

**body**
For more than one thing, add s: ticket → tickets. And drop a, because a means «one». The clip changes too: one thing — it is; more than one — they are.

**formula**
`They are` · `thing + s`

**examples** 🔊
| en | meaning | hi |
|---|---|---|
| They are tickets. | More than one ticket. | tickets |
| Are they coins? | You're not sure they're money. | coins |
| The cards are new. | Those cards are not old. | cards |
| They are towels. | More than one towel. | towels |
| They are spoons. | More than one spoon. | spoons |

**fix**
- ~~They are a tickets.~~ → They are tickets.
- ~~They are ticket.~~ → They are tickets.
- ~~It is tickets.~~ → They are tickets.

**drill · word_bank**
- task: A courier gives you some tickets. Say what they are.
- answer: `They` · `are` · `tickets`
- slot labels: who · clip · what
- extra tiles: `a` · `ticket`

## 06 · When you need es

**body**
If the word ends in s, sh or ch, you can't just add s. Add es, and the word gets one more beat: bus → buses, dish → dishes, watch → watches. You can hear it: «bus-iz», «dish-iz».

**examples** 🔊
| en | meaning | hi |
|---|---|---|
| They are buses. | More than one bus. | buses |
| Are they dishes? | Are they plates? | dishes |
| The watches are old. | Those watches are not new. | watches |
| They are glasses. | Things you drink from. | glasses |
| Are they brushes? | Things for hair or cleaning? | brushes |
| The benches are here. | The long seats are here. | benches |

**fix**
- ~~They are buss.~~ → They are buses.
- ~~The watchs are old.~~ → The watches are old.

---

## 07 · When y becomes ies

**body**
If the word ends in y and there is a consonant before it, change y to ies: battery → batteries, city → cities. It sounds almost the same, with a soft «z» at the end.

**examples** 🔊
| en | meaning | hi |
|---|---|---|
| They are batteries. | More than one battery. | batteries |
| Are they countries? | Are they names of countries? | countries |
| Where are the stories? | Where is the book with the stories? | stories |
| They are cities. | More than one city. | cities |

**fix**
- ~~They are batterys.~~ → They are batteries.
- ~~Where are the storys?~~ → Where are the stories?

**drill · choice**
- sentence: `Are they ___?`
- options: `countrys` · `countries` · `countryes`
- answer: `countries`
- why: There's a consonant (r) before y, so y becomes ies.

## 08 · this and that: near or far

**body**
Near you, in your hand — this. Far from you, something you point at — that. It's only about distance.

**examples** 🔊
| en | meaning | hi |
|---|---|---|
| This is a charger. | It's in my hand. | This |
| This is an adapter. | It's in my hand. | This |
| That is a screen. | It's over there on the wall. | That |
| Is this a remote? | The thing near you. | this |
| Is that a button? | The thing over there. | that |
| That is not a keyring. | The thing over there is something else. | That |

**drill · binary**
- question: The screen is on the far wall of the room. What do you say?
- A: `This is a screen.`
- B: `That is a screen.`
- correct: B
- explanation: The screen is far away, so that.

## 09 · these and those: more than one

**body**
For more than one thing, this becomes these and that becomes those. The clip is are. Near — these are. Far — those are.

**formula**
`one, near: this is` · `many, near: these are` · `one, far: that is` · `many, far: those are`

**examples** 🔊
| en | meaning | hi |
|---|---|---|
| These are shoes. | The shoes in front of me. | These |
| Those are blankets. | The blankets over there. | Those |
| Are these socks new? | The socks near you. | these |
| Those sheets are big. | The sheets over there are big. | Those |

**fix**
- ~~This are shoes.~~ → These are shoes.
- ~~These is shoes.~~ → These are shoes.

**drill · choice**
- sentence: `___ are shoes.` *(the box of shoes is right in front of you)*
- options: `This` · `These` · `Those`
- answer: `These`
- why: More than one shoe, and they are near: these.

## 10 · «This album», «those flowers»

**body**
this, that, these and those can go right before a thing: this album, those flowers. Then you don't need a. One thing — this or that. More than one — these or those, and the word has s.

**examples** 🔊
| en | meaning | hi |
|---|---|---|
| This album is big. | The album near me is big. | This |
| These frames are cheap. | The frames near me cost little. | These |
| Those flowers are fresh. | The flowers over there are fresh. | Those |
| This building is new. | The building we are in is new. | This |
| Is this lemon ripe? | Is the lemon near you ready to eat? | this |

**fix**
- ~~This a album is big.~~ → This album is big.
- ~~These frame are cheap.~~ → These frames are cheap.

## 11 · a or the

**body**
a is for something you mention for the first time, when the other person doesn't know which one yet: A cashier is here. the is for something you both already know about: The mistake is here.

**examples** 🔊
| en | meaning | hi |
|---|---|---|
| A cashier is here. | There is someone at the till. | A |
| This is a receipt. | A paper from the shop. | a |
| The mistake is here. | The mistake we talked about. | The |
| The handle is broken. | The handle of this door. | The |
| The seller is busy. | The seller here. | The |
| An item is missing. | One thing is not there. | An |

**tip**
First a, then the: This is a mistake. — Where? — The mistake is here. Now you both know which one.

**drill · choice**
- sentence: `___ mistake is here.` *(you and your friend already found the mistake on the receipt and point at it)*
- options: `A` · `An` · `The`
- answer: `The`
- why: You both know which mistake, so the.

## 12 · Things in general: no a, no the

**body**
When you talk about a kind of thing in general, not specific ones, just use the word with s: Books are heavy — any books. The magazines are new — these magazines that you both can see.

**examples** 🔊
| en | meaning | hi |
|---|---|---|
| Books are heavy. | All books, in general. | Books |
| Newspapers are cheap. | All newspapers, in general. | Newspapers |
| Wrenches are expensive. | All wrenches, in general. | Wrenches |
| The magazines are new. | These magazines here. | The |
| The stairs are narrow. | The stairs in this building. | The |

**fix**
- ~~The books are heavy.~~ *(about books in general)* → Books are heavy.

## 13 · Colour and other details go before the thing

**body**
The colour comes before the thing: a red cup, not a cup red. Colours never take s: black chairs, not blacks chairs. a or an depends on the first sound of the very next word: an olive coat, a yellow vase.

**formula**
`a / an / this / these` · `colour or detail` · `thing`

**examples** 🔊
| en | meaning | hi |
|---|---|---|
| It is a red cup. | One cup, and it is red. | red |
| It is an olive coat. | A coat the colour of olives. | an |
| These are black chairs. | Several chairs, all black. | black |
| This round table is heavy. | The round table here is heavy. | round |
| These metal boxes are heavy. | The boxes made of metal are heavy. | metal |
| This striped scarf is new. | The scarf with stripes is new. | striped |

**fix**
- ~~It is a cup red.~~ → It is a red cup.
- ~~These are blacks chairs.~~ → These are black chairs.
- ~~It is a olive coat.~~ → It is an olive coat.

**drill · word_bank**
- task: The seller asks which cup you want. Say it's a red cup.
- answer: `It` · `is` · `a` · `red` · `cup`
- slot labels: who · clip · one · colour · what
- extra tiles: `an` · `reds`

## 14 · Mistakes almost everyone makes

**fix**
- ~~It is chair.~~ → It is a chair.
- ~~It is a oven.~~ → It is an oven.
- ~~It is a hour.~~ → It is an hour.
- ~~They are a tickets.~~ → They are tickets.
- ~~This are shoes.~~ → These are shoes.
- ~~The books are heavy.~~ *(in general)* → Books are heavy.
- ~~It is a cup red.~~ → It is a red cup.

**tip**
Before you name a thing, ask two questions. One or more? One — a/an, this/that. More — s at the end, these/those, are. And: does the other person know which one? If yes — the.

**drill · binary**
- question: The shirts are in front of you. Which is correct?
- A: `This shirts are clean.`
- B: `These shirts are clean.`
- correct: B
- explanation: More than one shirt, and they're near: these.

## 15 · What to take into practice

**body**
One thing — a or an by the first sound. More than one — add s, no a. Near — this / these; far — that / those. Something you both know — the. In general — no the. Colour before the thing: a red cup.

**examples** 🔊
| en | meaning | hi |
|---|---|---|
| It is an hour. | Sixty minutes. | an |
| They are batteries. | More than one battery. | batteries |
| Those flowers are fresh. | The flowers over there. | Those |
| The mistake is here. | The mistake we talked about. | The |
| It is a red cup. | One cup, and it is red. | red |
